Notation and units

A change is written ΔX = Xf − Xi. U, H, Q and W are expressed in joules. A molar enthalpy is expressed in J·mol⁻¹ or kJ·mol⁻¹. The symbol ° denotes standard states; the temperature must still be specified.

Equation Application
1 bar = 10⁵ Pa ; 1 L = 10⁻³ m³ 1 Pa·m³ = 1 J ; 1 bar·L = 100 J
1 kJ = 1 000 J Express all contributions in the same unit before adding them.
T/K = θ/°C + 273.15 A temperature difference of 1 °C equals 1 K.
R = 8.314 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ Use T in kelvins with R.
p° = 1 bar ; c° = 1 mol·L⁻¹ Standard conventions.

Describing a system

Exchanges with the surroundings

Open: matter and energy
Closed: energy, but no matter
Isolated: neither matter nor energy

The definitions concern the exchanges that are possible.

Parameters

: m, V, n
: T, p, concentration, mole fraction

An extensive parameter is proportional to the amount of substance. An intensive parameter is independent of it.

Review the exchange diagram

Amounts of substance and gases: n = m/M ; m = ρV ; n = cV ; pV = nRT ; xᵢ = nᵢ/ntot ; pᵢ = xᵢp. Extent of reaction: nᵢ = nᵢ,₀ + νᵢξ; for a complete reaction, ξmax is the smallest nᵢ,₀/|νᵢ| among the reactants.

State functions and processes

Integrating an exact differential

∫ifdU=Uf−Ui∫ifdH=Hf−Hi

U and H are state functions: their changes do not depend on the path. Over a cycle, their changes are zero. Q and W depend on the path: ∫δQ = Q and ∫δW = W.

Processes at a constant parameter

: W = 0 and ΔU = QV
, ideal gas of fixed composition: ΔU = 0 and W = −Q

System properties and exchanges with the surroundings

: Q = 0 and ΔU = W
: Q = W = ΔU = 0

The balance applies to a closed system with pressure–volume work only and negligible changes in macroscopic mechanical energy. A process with no heat transfer is also called adiabatic. A system with adiabatic walls can exchange work; an isolated system cannot.

Cycle: a sequence of processes returning the system to its initial state. The path is closed; ΔUcycle = ΔHcycle = 0 and Qcycle + Wcycle = 0.

Calculating work

General expression

δW=−pextdVW=−∫ViVfpext(V)dV

Work depends on the external pressure. Expansion against a positive pressure gives W < 0; compression gives W > 0.

Monobaric process

W = −pext(Vf − Vi)

If external pressure is constant, it can be taken outside the integral. Isobaric means constant system pressure; constant external pressure is a separate condition.

Explore rectangles and relate area to an antiderivative

Ideal gas, fixed amount, reversible isothermal process

W = −nRT ln(Vf/Vi) = nRT ln(pf/pi)

We use pext = p = nRT/V in the reversible limit. An isothermal process alone does not justify this substitution. At constant volume, W = 0. At constant pressure with p = pext, W = −nR(Tf − Ti).

Heat capacities

Cp = (∂H/∂T)p
C = n Cmolar = m cspecific

System heat capacity: J·K⁻¹; molar: J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹; specific: J·g⁻¹·K⁻¹ or J·kg⁻¹·K⁻¹. Choose the mass unit matching the given value.

Temperature change without a phase transition

Q = C ΔT = n Cmolar ΔT = m cspecific ΔT
At constant pressure: ΔH = ∑ᵢ nᵢ Cp,m,i (Tf−Ti)

These expressions assume fixed composition, no reaction or phase transition, and constant heat capacities over the interval. For a mixture, sum the contributions of its constituents.

Phase changes

Phase transition

ΔH = n Δphase transitionH°
Qtotal = ∑ Qheating + ∑ Qphase transition

n is the amount transformed. Use the molar enthalpy of the phase transition at the temperature of this step.

Latent heat: heat exchanged during a phase transition at constant temperature, for a pure substance at constant pressure. For a mass-specific value, L is in J·kg⁻¹ (or J·g⁻¹):

Qp = m L = n Δphase transitionH
L = Δphase transitionH / M

m, M and L must use consistent mass units. Lfus and Lvap are positive; for freezing and condensation at the same temperature, Qp = −m L. During the plateau, heat received changes the phase rather than increasing temperature: m c ΔT is not used for this step.

Notation: Δ indicates a change; H denotes enthalpy; subscripts r, f, comb, fus or vap identify the process; ° denotes standard states; T is temperature in kelvins. ΔH is in J; ΔrH is in J·mol⁻¹.

Reaction enthalpies

Definition and extent of reaction

ΔrH = (∂H/∂ξ)T,p
ΔH ≈ ξ ΔrH°

For the reaction contribution at constant temperature and pressure. This expression assumes zero initial extent and a reaction enthalpy approximated by a constant standard value. In the Ellingham approximation, ΔrH° is treated as constant over the temperature interval.

Dissociation, ionisation, electron attachment

A–B(g) → A(g) + B(g) : ΔdissH° > 0
M(g) → M⁺(g) + e⁻ : ΔionH° > 0
M(g) + e⁻ → M⁻(g) : ΔAEH°

For bond formation from gaseous atoms, use the negative of the bond dissociation enthalpy.

Review definitions of specific reaction enthalpies

Phases: species can cancel in a sum of equations only if they have the same formula and physical state. If needed, add phase-transition or dissolution enthalpies. Dissolution A(s) → A(aq) differs from gaseous bond dissociation.

Elemental substance: contains only one chemical element, for example H₂, O₂ or C(graphite). A compound contains several elements. Formation produces one mole of compound from the reference elemental substances.

Combustion : H₂O(l) unless otherwise stated in this pathway. Molar combustion enthalpy refers to one mole of fuel; the water phase must match the data used. Bond enthalpies concern gaseous species, separating the bond contribution from intermolecular interactions.

Dissolution of an ionic solid: lattice dissociation and ion solvation; in water, hydration. Dissolving a molecular compound does not necessarily form ions.

Hess cycles

Using formation enthalpies

ΔrH° = ∑ νᵢ ΔfH°(products) − ∑ νᵢ ΔfH°(reactants)

The coefficients in both sums are the positive coefficients of the balanced equation. The phases and temperature of the data must match the reaction. ΔfH° is zero for an element in its standard reference state.

Relationship between ΔrH° and ΔrU° for bomb calorimetry

Combining reactions

ΔrH°target = ∑ⱼ kⱼ ΔrH°ⱼ

Reversing a reaction changes the sign of its enthalpy. Multiplying an equation by k multiplies its enthalpy by k. Adding the equations must give the target reaction.

Calorimetric energy balances

Sum of contributions

∑ Qp,i = 0
Qcalo + Qheating + Qphase transition + Qreaction = 0

An adiabatic calorimetric assembly at constant pressure in this model. Include only the processes that actually occur. The constituents may exchange heat with one another.

Calibration using two water samples

Ccalo(Tf−T₁) + m₁c(Tf−T₁) + m₂c(Tf−T₂) = 0

The calorimeter and cold water start at T₁; the hot water starts at T₂. Rearrange this equation to obtain Ccalo.

Heat of reaction

Qreaction = −∑ Qother contributions

Calculate each temperature change separately. In the Zn/Cu²⁺ exercise, the sum includes the calorimeter, solution and zinc.

Partial or complete melting

Ice at 0 °C, water and calorimeter initially at Tᵢ > 0 °C: compare (Ccal + mwatercwater)Tᵢ with miceLfus. If the first value is smaller, Tf = 0 °C and mmelted = Qavailable/Lfus. Otherwise all the ice melts; add the heating of the resulting water if Tf > 0 °C.

Molar values: at constant pressure, ΔrH ≈ Qreaction/ξ; at constant volume with pressure–volume work only, ΔrU ≈ QV, reaction/ξ. The sign is that of the heat received by the reacting system.

Mathematical guide: antiderivatives and integrals

The integral adds contributions over an interval. Rectangles provide an approximation. If F′ = f, an antiderivative gives the exact calculation:
